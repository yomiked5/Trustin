## Product and audience

Market to parents, initially of children ages 4–5. Child discovery happens on Apple Watch; the Parent Dock is on iPhone. The native app is in development/testing. The website offers prepared samples without live AI, microphone access, a child account, or a physical device connection.

Mission: to open a world of knowledge and discovery to every child, while keeping parents at the center of their learning journey. Conviction: the future of learning should not come at the expense of childhood.

The three commitments are discovery for children, meaningful parent participation, and childhood free from advertising, sponsored answers, purchase pressure, or manipulative engagement. Parent preferences add to essential safeguards. A family preference cannot make harmful content permissible. AI may be wrong; human relationships remain central.

Do not invent efficacy, testimonials, kid approval, compliance, live settings, complete monitoring, release dates, prices, or App Store availability. Wider age choices in source do not expand the announced release scope. Activity counts are not learning scores.

## Product versus presentation

Keep actual product labels, readable answers, feedback, and errors within the product. Keep website explanation, tutorial navigation, sample labels, marketing links, and rationale outside its viewport.

The homepage devices are source-grounded HTML/CSS renderings, not native runtime or pixel-identical screenshots. The original Trustin art is unchanged. iOS fonts, symbols, hardware, and examples are web approximations. Example activity data is disclosed outside the phone. No account is connected.

The working browser demo remains an adaptation. Its harmless Moon preference is illustrative, not a native topic category. Its listening controls affect browser recordings only. The phone shows the current iOS hierarchy with local functional sample controls. Final release UI may change.

### Gesture and geometry contract

- A touch starting inside the device belongs to its sample UI. It cannot change the surrounding explanation.
- A horizontal swipe on the surrounding stage changes the explanation; right goes backward, left goes forward. Clicks, arrows and selectors remain available.
- Vertical movement remains page or panel scrolling; pinch zoom remains available. A swipe cannot accidentally activate the button under the finger.
- Device and description have separate fixed slots. Changing text, chapter or disclosure cannot move, stretch or resize the device.
- Viewport sizing reserves room for heading, controls, descriptions and even the Watch strap. Narrow or short screens get a readable fallback instead of trapped scrolling.
- Sticky sections are bounded by normal document flow and include direct skip links. There is no wheel interception or forced sequence.

State is memory-only. The parent phone uses clearly fictional samples, not analytics from the visitor’s Watch play. Counts are not learning scores. Reload/reset clears the Watch state. Nothing is sent to a physical Watch. Native code, permissions and release status are unchanged.

## Release continuity

The owner’s October 14 screenshots exposed a failed v1.7 experience: new HTML paired with old CSS/JS produced giant icons, an obsolete demo error and a large Parent phone with missing explanations. Live inspection found root scripts/styles cached for four hours while HTML revalidated. Passing source tests and a green deployment did not detect the returning-visitor mismatch.

All page styles and scripts now use one content-addressed `/releases/<hash>/` directory; relative imports remain inside it. A source change invalidates the whole presentation dependency set. Old release folders remain available for open documents. Mutable HTML/root asset URLs revalidate; only versioned release files are immutable. Preparing and checking this release is a publishing gate. The Parent Dock’s stable geometry and first explanation are present in the HTML before enhancement; a no-JavaScript stylesheet restores all explanations to ordinary flow.

For future visual work, verify delivery coherence and first-load layout as well as controller behavior. A successful deployment and automated test count cannot establish an acceptable mobile presentation.
